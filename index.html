The Problem

The world changed faster than the curriculum.

Talent is not scarce at institutions like ETH Zurich — but the pathways to apply it across disciplines are fragmented. Two structural gaps hold ambitious students back.

01

Education vs. evolving skills

Artificial intelligence and data-driven methods are reshaping finance and consulting faster than academic curricula can adapt. Domain expertise alone is no longer sufficient — adaptability, quantitative fluency, and interdisciplinary reasoning have become essential.

02

Fragmented talent & access

A physicist with a passion for markets, or a computer scientist drawn to investment management, often has no clear pathway in. In Switzerland and the DACH region, opportunities exist but remain scattered across institutions, companies, and disciplines.

Our Goal

A new generation of professionals — fluent in both finance and technology.

We aim to redefine how financial and technical expertise are developed, integrated, and applied. By combining interdisciplinary education, independent research, and practical industry engagement, we cultivate professionals equipped with both financial insight and technical proficiency.

Our central objective is the reciprocal transfer of expertise: equipping finance and economics students with advanced computational competencies, while giving technically oriented students a deep understanding of markets, economic principles, and investment methodology.

“The question is not whether traditional education is losing its value — but whether its structures alone can prepare students for a future that is increasingly difficult to anticipate.”

Strategic Objectives

Four pillars, one integrated framework.

01

Academic Collaboration

A cross-institutional network connecting students from leading universities — with initial efforts targeting ETH Zurich, the University of St. Gallen, and LMU Munich. Joint projects and events dissolve disciplinary and institutional boundaries.

02

Reciprocal Education

A student-led programme for the mutual exchange of knowledge. Technical students contribute mathematics, programming, and AI; finance students introduce markets, economics, and investment — through courses and interactive workshops.

03

Applied Research

Bridging theory and practice through extended case studies, trading competitions, and independent financial research. Multi-day challenges with industry partners tackle real problems in banking, asset management, and consulting.

04

Industry & Talent

Strategic relationships with financial institutions, consulting firms, and technology companies across the DACH region — plus a rotational Mentor-Cycle connecting students with experienced professionals every month.

Long-Term Vision

From a student club to an independent institution.

Expand the network

Grow the university network and institutional partnerships to become an established part of the DACH early-career talent pipeline.

Research & investment division

Dedicated analyst teams covering traditional and digital assets — publishing market reports and developing proprietary investment strategies.

A public platform

Research publications, interviews, podcasts, and digital media making interdisciplinary perspectives accessible to a broader audience.

Institutional independence

A distinct identity and brand built on intellectual rigour, interdisciplinary excellence, and professional integrity.
